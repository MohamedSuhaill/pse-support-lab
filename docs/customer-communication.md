# Customer communication guidelines

## Principles

1. **Acknowledge fast.** A short, human reply within the SLA beats a perfect reply that is late.
2. **Own it.** Use "I" and "we". Give your name. The customer should know who is looking after them.
3. **Plain language.** Write for a finance person, not an engineer. If you must use a technical term (ASP, Peppol, TRN, BR-CO-15), explain it in one short sentence.
4. **No blame.** Not the customer, not a colleague, not "the system". If the customer made a mistake, explain how to fix it kindly. If we made a mistake, say so.
5. **Only promise what you control.** No unverified causes, no fix dates you have not agreed with engineering. "I will update you by 14:00" is a promise you can keep; "it will be fixed by 14:00" usually is not.
6. **Give them something to do — or tell them to wait.** A workaround, a safe next step, or a clear "please do not re-upload until I confirm".
7. **Always state the next update time.** And keep it, even if there is no news.
8. **Answer every question they asked.** Number your answers if they numbered their questions.

## Structures

**Acknowledgement**

1. Thank them and restate the problem in one sentence (shows you understood).
2. What you are doing now.
3. Any immediate safe advice (what to do / not to do).
4. When they will hear from you next.

**Update**

1. What we know now (facts, not guesses).
2. What we are doing next.
3. What it means for them (impact, workaround).
4. Next update time.

**Resolution**

1. What happened, in plain words.
2. What we did / what they need to do.
3. How to confirm it is fixed.
4. What we are doing to stop it happening again.
5. An invitation to reply if anything is still wrong.

## Do and don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "This is a known issue." | "We have seen this with other customers and our engineers are working on it. Here is what you can do in the meantime." |
| "You uploaded the wrong file." | "The file has the invoice date in a format the portal can't read. Here is how to change it in two steps." |
| "It should work now." | "I have checked and your 12 invoices now show Accepted. Could you confirm on your side?" |
| "Engineering will fix it ASAP." | "I have passed this to our engineering team with all the details. I will update you by 15:00 today." |
| Long paragraphs of log output. | One or two sentences of explanation; attach details only if they asked. |
| "Please be patient." | "I understand this is holding up your month-end close. Here is what we are doing about it." |
